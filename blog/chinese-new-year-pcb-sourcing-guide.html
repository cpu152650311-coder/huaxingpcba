Chinese New Year PCB Orders:
2027 Factory Shutdown Timeline & Procurement Checklist

Every January, the world's largest electronics supply chain pauses for 1-4 weeks. Buyers who plan in Q3 and Q4 ship on schedule — everyone else waits until March.

Blog / Chinese New Year PCB Planning

Chinese New Year (Spring Festival) is the single biggest recurring disruption in the global electronics supply chain. For 2027, the holiday falls on February 6 (Year of the Goat), but its supply chain impact spans six weeks: workers travel home in the weeks before, factories wind down production, and crews return gradually afterward. For PCB and PCBA buyers, this means lead times that stretch from 2-3 weeks to 5-8 weeks, freight capacity that evaporates, and component prices that firm up — unless you plan ahead.

This guide gives you the 2027 shutdown timeline, the real impact on lead time and cost, and a month-by-month procurement checklist. We run a Shenzhen factory with 8 SMT lines, and we publish our holiday schedule every year so customers can plan — the timeline below reflects how an actual mid-size PCB manufacturer operates, not a generic worst case. Why Chinese New Year Disrupts the PCB Supply Chain Every Year

The Spring Festival is the world's largest annual human migration — hundreds of millions of workers travel from coastal factory cities back to their home provinces. The electronics industry is concentrated in Shenzhen, Guangzhou, and the Pearl River Delta, so the effect on PCB and assembly capacity is outsized:

1

Production staff go home, and not all of them come back

Factories operate with a fraction of their normal workforce for 2-4 weeks. Skilled operators — SMT technicians, AOI inspectors, test engineers — are the hardest to replace, so quality-sensitive processes (fine-pitch assembly, X-ray inspection) recover slowest after the holiday.

2

Component suppliers shut down too

It is not just your CM. Substrate makers, laminate mills, connector factories, and distributors all close or run skeleton crews. Even if your assembly line is open, missing parts can stall a build for weeks. Supply chain risk of this kind is the subject of our supply chain risk management guide.

3

Freight and logistics tighten before the holiday, not after

Exporters rush shipments out before the break, so ports and forwarders hit peak congestion 3-4 weeks before CNY. Container space gets scarce, air freight rates climb, and customs processing slows. Shipping terms and their risks are covered in our Incoterms guide.

How CNY Affects Lead Times and Costs

Plan on these three effects, and budget for them:

1

Lead times stretch by 2-4 weeks

A standard 5-7 day PCB batch and 7-10 day PCBA run becomes a 4-8 week program when ordered too close to the holiday. The factory calendar, not the production clock, sets your delivery date. Our lead time guide covers the normal levers; during CNY the only lever is ordering earlier.

2

Component prices firm up in Q4

Distributors and manufacturers build inventory ahead of CNY, and short-supply parts — memory, power ICs, specific connectors — see price pressure from November. Locking long-lead components before the holiday is a standard cost-protection move.

3

Freight costs spike 20-50% in the pre-holiday window

Sea freight container rates and air cargo rates both climb as exporters front-run the shutdown. Booking 3-4 weeks ahead and accepting a slightly earlier ship date usually beats waiting for a cheaper rate that never comes. Import cost structures are covered in our import duty and tariff guide.

The Procurement Checklist: Lock Orders Before the Rush

The buyers who sail through CNY place their bets in Q3 and Q4. Here is the month-by-month playbook:

1

Now to November: freeze designs and forecast volume

Any board respin or BOM change planned for January will land in the shutdown window. Freeze designs, finalize BOMs, and give your CM a volume forecast so they can reserve capacity. Design changes during the holiday are the classic source of CNY horror stories.

2

November-December: order long-lead components

Place orders for anything with 8+ week lead times before the end of the year. If you use a turnkey assembly service, the CM's procurement team handles this — confirm they are staging components for your Q1 builds.

3

December: place the production orders

Orders placed in December with a mid-January completion target fit inside the pre-holiday window. Confirm the CM's last shipping date and get it in writing. A blanket order covering Q1 volumes lets the factory plan raw material purchasing around the shutdown.

4

Mid-January: stock a buffer, book freight

Ship a safety buffer of finished boards before the last shipping date, and book freight 3-4 weeks ahead. If you run rolling stock, consider VMI so the CM holds inventory for you across the holiday — you draw stock in February without waiting for production.

5

During the holiday: set expectations internally

Tell your own team and customers that nothing ships for 1-2 weeks. Factories keep a skeleton crew for emergencies — urgent queries get responses, new work does not. Payment terms agreed in advance avoid invoice surprises across the break; see our payment terms guide.

What a Good CM Does During CNY

A factory that handles CNY professionally is a strong long-term partner. Five behaviors to look for:

1

Publishes the holiday schedule early

The shutdown window, last order date, last shipping date, and reopening date should be communicated by November — not discovered in January. We publish ours to all customers with the same formality as a price list.

2

Pre-builds and pre-orders raw materials

Laminate, copper foil, solder paste, and common components bought before the holiday mean production restarts at speed in February. A CM that treats CNY as a planning event, not a surprise, keeps your Q1 schedule intact.

3

Maintains a communication channel

A named contact (often management, since operators are away) answers urgent queries through the holiday. Silence for two weeks is a red flag — it usually means nobody is steering the ship.

4

Runs a skeleton crew for urgent work

Critical rework, test failures, and emergency samples get handled by a small on-site team. Don't expect volume production — but do expect a defined process for what qualifies as urgent.

5

Honors the pre-holiday commitment

The real test is whether boards promised before the last shipping date actually ship. A CM that quietly slips CNY orders by a month without notice is telling you how they will handle every future crisis. Relationship evaluation criteria are in our supplier scorecard guide.

Key Takeaway: Chinese New Year is a planning problem, not a crisis. Freeze designs by November, order long-lead components before December, place production orders in December, ship a buffer in mid-January, and confirm the factory's shutdown schedule in writing. Buyers who do this pay normal prices and ship on time.
